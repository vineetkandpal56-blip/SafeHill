RISK ANALYSIS

What The Historical Records Tell Us

Historical information can help identify recurring patterns and improve preparedness.

🏔️

Recurring Slope Instability

Repeated reports of landslide and road instability indicate the importance of continuous monitoring around vulnerable mountain stretches.

🌧️

Weather Connection

Heavy rainfall can increase the likelihood of slope failure, erosion and road damage in vulnerable Himalayan terrain.

🚨

Early Warning Need

Combining historical records with live weather and location information can help users understand risk conditions earlier.

DISASTER RESPONSE

Recommended Response Workflow

01 — Detect

Monitor weather, rainfall, location and known danger zones.

02 — Analyse

Compare current conditions with predefined risk thresholds.

03 — Warn

Provide clear warning messages and emergency alerts to users.

04 — Act

Encourage users to avoid dangerous areas and follow official instructions.

📚 Data & Source Note

This page is intended as a historical information module for the SafeHill college project. Historical records should be updated only after verification from reliable news reports, government departments or official disaster management records.

Exact rainfall totals, number of affected people, financial damage and exact blockage duration are marked as unavailable where reliable verified figures are not currently available.
